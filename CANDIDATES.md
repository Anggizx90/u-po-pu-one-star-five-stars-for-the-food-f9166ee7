# Candidate sentences — One Star, Five Stars for the Food

Judged against the house criteria, in order:
1. Is a one-star review of Earth from an alien tourist that still slips in a compliment about the food.
2. Sounds like a real alien visitor: distinct voice, outsider's bafflement, a grievance worth one star.
3. Lands the joke: complaint and food compliment both hit, the turn is funny rather than merely stated.
4. One sentence, inside the character limit, every word earning its place.
5. Feels fresh: an original angle, not generic complaints.

Scores are c1/c2/c3/c4/c5 out of 5. All candidates are one sentence with exactly
one terminal full stop, no abbreviations, no semicolon tricks. Lengths verified
by script (Python `len`, same as JS `.length` for these ASCII texts).

## Round 4 — final form: name Earth, first-person review voice

The "One star:" label never says "Earth". Criterion 1 is a one-star review
*of Earth*, so the winning line was recast as a real review verdict:

- O1f (180): I give Earth one star because humans fence off land they did not create, then argue about who owns which side, but the xiao long bao handed over that fence were worth the trespass.
- O1g (170): One star for Earth: humans fence off land they did not create, then argue about who owns which side, but the xiao long bao handed over that fence were worth the trespass.
- A1f (182): I give Earth one star because humans celebrate one lap around their star by setting the sky on fire, then complain about the noise, but the street satay was worth the hearing damage.
- O1e (169): One star: humans fence off land they did not create, then argue about who owns which side of Earth, but the xiao long bao handed over that fence were worth the trespass.

O1f beats O1d: it names Earth explicitly and speaks in the first person, so
it wins criteria 1 and 2 outright. O1f beats O1g: the full review sentence
("I give Earth one star because…") is a stronger alien-tourist voice than a
label. O1f beats A1f (margin 1): C1 tied; C2 roughly even (A1f's "one lap
around their star" is the single most alien phrase, O1f's grievance is
weightier); C3 decides — "worth the trespass" integrates the food into the
grievance, where "worth the hearing damage" only prices it.

## Winner — O1f (180 characters)

> I give Earth one star because humans fence off land they did not create, then argue about who owns which side, but the xiao long bao handed over that fence were worth the trespass.

Why it wins: a specific grievance (humans fencing land they did not create)
that only an outsider would find baffling; a first-person review voice that
names Earth and the one star; a named dish, not the generic word "food"; and
a turn where the compliment *is* the trespass — the food is passed over the
very fence being complained about, so the praise resolves the complaint
instead of sitting next to it. No candidate beat it in any round.
